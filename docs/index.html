Step 1 · The pipeline

From a game idea to a market percentile

The Game Launch IDSS answers one question: given only what a developer decides before launch (price, platforms, genres, tags, Steam features), where would the game rank among 27,075 Steam games, and which single change would move it up the most? This lab walks through the exact computation, one step at a time.

Step 2 · The data

27,075 Steam games, and a very skewed target

steam.csv lists every game with its release date, platforms, categories, genres, SteamSpy tags, price and an owners range (for example 20000-50000). The loader converts each range to its midpoint. Because owners are only known as 13 ranges, the target is interval-censored: two games in the same range are indistinguishable.

Step 4 · Feature mapping

Your form becomes a vector of 91 numbers

IntelligenceEngine._build_ranker_features converts the form into a dictionary; _predict_rank then builds a row with the model's 91 columns, filling every column it did not receive with 0. Cells light up when the value is non-zero. Hover a cell for its column name.

Which inputs actually reach the model?

Hidden defaults: the form has no field for release year, game age or studio size, so the mapping always uses release_year = 2024, game_age_days = 100 and dev_game_count = 1. Publisher columns stay 0, a value never seen in training (every real game has at least one publisher game).
Step 5 · Gradient boosting

150 small trees, each correcting the previous ones

The ranker is an XGBRegressor. Its prediction is a constant plus the sum of the outputs of 150 regression trees: score = base + f1(x) + ... + f150(x). Tree m is fitted to the gradient of the squared error of the first m - 1 trees (for squared error, simply the residuals), and its output is shrunk by the learning rate 0.08.

    Step 6 · Rank target and Spearman

    Learning the order, not the owner count

    The target is not owners but rankdata(owners) / n (ranking_integration.py, _train_ensemble): each training game is replaced by its rank, scaled to (0, 1]. Ties share their average rank, so the 69% of training games in the 0 to 20k range all receive 0.345. The quality metric is therefore a rank metric: Spearman's ρ, the Pearson correlation computed on ranks.

    Real held-out games: tier vs predicted score

    800 random games of the 5,415-game test split. Amber line: median score per tier.

    Why Spearman and not R²?

    Distort the scores of the same 800 games and compare the two correlations with the actual owners tier.

    --
    Spearman ρ (ranks)
    --
    Pearson r (raw values)

    Any monotone distortion leaves Spearman unchanged, because ranks do not change; Pearson moves. Noise, which reorders games, lowers both. A percentile only depends on order, so Spearman is the metric that matches how the score is used.

    Step 8 · What-if engine

    13 counterfactual changes, re-scored one at a time

    get_improvements copies your feature dictionary, sets one feature to a target value, recomputes the percentile and keeps the change when the gain exceeds 0.5 percentile points. Candidates whose feature is already present, or whose dependency is missing, are skipped. The engine shows the six largest gains. Click apply to make the change through the form and see the real effect.

    Where the confidence labels come from

    At training time, _validate_improvements checks seven features on the test split. For each, it compares games with and without the feature and evaluates two booleans: is the model's mean predicted rank higher with the feature? and is the actual median owners strictly higher with the feature? Equal booleans give High, different booleans give Low. Features with fewer than 50 test games in either group are not validated, and every non-validated candidate is labelled Medium.

    Why three features come out Low with a lift of 0%: about 67% of test games sit in the lowest owners range, so the median owners of both groups is 10,000 (the midpoint of 0 to 20k) and the lift is exactly zero. "Low" here means "the median cannot see a difference", not "the feature hurts".

    Step 10 · How good is it?

    Measured performance, and what it does not mean

    Held-out games: predicted vs actual percentile

    For each owners range, the most-reviewed game of the test split. Actual percentile = average rank of the game's owners range among the 5,415 test games.

    Read the numbers with care. The 0.725 (bootstrap 95% interval 0.712 to 0.738) is measured on a random split, where a game's age and release year tell the model how long the game has been accumulating owners. Retraining the same recipe on a temporal split (train on games released before 2018, test on 2018 and later) gives a Spearman of 0.432, which is the better estimate for a game that has not launched yet. Associations are not causal effects: "games with Trading Cards rank higher" does not prove that adding Trading Cards causes more owners. See docs/11_limitations.md.
